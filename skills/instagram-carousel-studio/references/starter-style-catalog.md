# Four Neutral Starter Styles

These are mechanism-first starting points, not brand identities. Replace the palette, logo, type, and imagery with user-cleared inputs.

## A — Editorial Contrast

- Strong typographic hierarchy with one oversized phrase.
- Asymmetric grid, generous breathing room, one editorial crop or abstract visual.
- High contrast, restrained accent color, minimal depth.
- Best for opinions, reframes, checklists, and strategy lessons.
- Avoid magazine imitation, decorative paragraphs, and tiny captions.

## B — Dark System

- Deep layered background with luminous data, network, or interface structures.
- One bright focal object and clear content panels.
- Depth from light, blur, and translucency rather than many flat icons.
- Best for AI systems, workflows, models, and technical frameworks.
- Avoid sci-fi clutter, fake dashboards, excessive English labels, and neon everywhere.

## C — Product Visual

- Large tactile or dimensional hero object that makes the pain or transformation visible.
- Content-first headline remains dominant; the hero supports the thesis.
- Layered glass, soft volume, or clean product-render lighting.
- Best for tools, before/after, system complexity, and conceptual metaphors.
- Avoid image-only beauty shots, generated logo substitutes, and unreadable embedded text.

## D — Tactile Explainer

- Modular cards, diagrams, annotated objects, or physical-model metaphors.
- Clear sequence and visible relationships between concepts.
- Material may be clay, fabric, plastic, metal, or mixed digital layers; paper is optional, never default.
- Best for steps, comparisons, decision trees, and learning frameworks.
- Avoid flat-icon grids, scrapbooking for its own sake, and decoration without meaning.

## Selection Rule

Compare all four with the same approved cover copy. If a user reference establishes a fifth direction, create a project-local candidate rather than mutating these bundled Styles.
