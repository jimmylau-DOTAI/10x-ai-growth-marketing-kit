# Tone extraction

## What belongs in TONE.md

Distil repeatable language behaviour:

- speaker／brand role and reader relationship;
- Hook mechanisms and opening tension;
- sentence length, rhythm, paragraph breathing, and use of questions;
- vocabulary preferences, technical-term handling, and language mix;
- explanation, story, proof, objection, and CTA patterns;
- allowed variation by platform or content job;
- named failure patterns a reviewer can actually see.

Write rules as observable behaviour and include the reason when a rule could be over-applied.

## What does not belong

Do not store exact source sentences, private stories, a creator's identity, current topic, product claims, campaign facts, names, prices, dates, links, or platform limits that may change. Keep those in the source receipt or Content Job.

Do not promise to reproduce a living creator's personal voice. Extract broad communication mechanisms and make the candidate identity-safe. Rights or attribution uncertainty remains visible in the evidence record.

## Evidence and testing

Classify each Tone rule as user-confirmed, observed, inferred, or open. One source can create a candidate, not a proven Tone.

Test with at least two different topics or formats. A passing result should remain recognisable without copying source wording, inventing experiences, or forcing every post into the same template.

Keep one baseline where practical. Review clarity, voice consistency, factual fidelity, phrase overlap, platform fit, and whether the Tone leaves enough room for the current content job.
